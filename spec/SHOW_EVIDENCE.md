# SHOW_EVIDENCE.md — Observational Evidence Log: Light Use in Professional House Shows

## TL;DR

**No fabricated cue tables are presented here.** Genuine [LOGGED] frame-
counting was not possible for this agent, so this log is built on a
[DOCUMENTED] backbone (trade press, LD/programmer interviews, production-
company and manufacturer case studies) plus explicitly labelled [INFERRED]
interpretation. The single most valuable calibration column — simultaneous
"hues live," and every per-bar timing statistic — **is not countable from
the written record and was not gathered from footage**; it is flagged as an
open task, not estimated.

**The strongest defensible finding is about control philosophy, not
counts:** top pro house/EDM shows are built on a *busked/manual baseline
with timecode layered on only for sub-frame accents*, and the published
design discipline is explicit restraint ("not using everything for every
song") plus repetition of looks when musical sections repeat. There is no
documented evidence that continuous per-quarter-note kick-sync is the
professional norm; the evidence points the other way.

**School split, honestly:** on nearly every footage-dependent statistic the
two schools are *indistinguishable in the available written record* — that
itself is a finding. The only robustly documented differences: big-room/
pop-EDM (Zedd, Chainsmokers) carries larger blinder/strobe/video counts and
narrative color; tech-house (Summit, Dolla, Lake) design language emphasizes
restraint and monochrome-toward-white.

## PART 3 — AGGREGATE STATISTICS

Each item states the number (or its absence), sample size, spread, and
school split.

**1. Look hold time.** *Not countable from available footage.* [INFERRED]
Documented practice cues looks to musical phrase boundaries (4/8/16 bars).
A written review of an Anyma (melodic techno) show in Dubai states "every
four or eight bars something shifted, never randomly" (Designoor). At 128
BPM a 4-bar phrase ≈ 7.5 s and 8-bar ≈ 15 s. **Sample: 1 documented
review.** Median / 10th–90th percentile and "shortest intentional hold": not
measurable without footage. School split: not measurable.

**2. Simultaneous hue count.** *Not countable — no hue-count integers exist
in any source.* [INFERRED] Design language for tech-house/techno is
documented as restrained and monochrome-leaning through builds; big-room
uses more color juxtaposition. Mode and "max sustained >4 bars": not
gathered. **This is the highest-value calibration column and it must be
obtained from footage.**

**3. Kick-following prevalence.** *Not countable without footage, but
documented evidence bears on the question indirectly.* [DOCUMENTED] Per
LD/director Stevie Hernandez to PLSN ("Zedd Orbit Tour," 2019): "It's a
very cue-heavy show. This is also the first tour we used timecode. The past
tours and all other shows and festivals were all manually operated in all
departments," with timecode later layered only for "extra elements …
not humanly possible to hit manually." A human operator busking a 128 BPM
set cannot hand-fire every quarter note (7.5 quarter-notes/sec range), so
**continuous per-kick intensity pulsing is not the professional manual
baseline.** No source documents continuous quarter-note pulsing as
standard. **Sample: 2 LD statements (Zedd Orbit + Robe True Colors).
Verdict: continuous kick-sync is not evidenced as the norm; phrase/downbeat
accenting is.**

**4. Pre-drop blackout.** *Duration in beats not quantified in any rigorous
source.* [DOCUMENTED, generic] Club-lighting technique writeups describe
the move explicitly: "Kill every fixture for half a beat before the drop
lands" (Topprosound). That "half a beat" is trade-blog phrasing, not a
measured distribution. Frequency of builds ending in blackout: not counted.

**5. Drop-hit hold.** *Not countable without footage.* No documented value.

**6. Laser duty cycle.** *Discrete moments/hour, minimum spacing, and
duty-cycle fraction: NOT found in any source; not countable.*
[DOCUMENTED presence only] John Summit (2025) and Zedd carried dedicated
laser programmers; every Chainsmokers tour carried lasers (Memories
per-pod: "two Lightline Lasertechnik lasers … for audience scanning").
Festival scale is large: EDC Las Vegas 2026's kineticFIELD (headliners
including John Summit and Chris Lake) ran "1,400 light fixtures, 26 lasers,
and 33 pyro cannons" on a 440 ft × 100 ft stage, per Insomniac founder
Pasquale Rotella (via Decibel Events/Stage Hoppers). Club vs festival:
manual/busked lasers are documented as more common in club/DJ settings;
timecoded laser cues dominate at festival scale.

**7. Strobe usage.** *Burst durations, gaps, and rate: not countable without
footage.* [DOCUMENTED] Strobe/blinder fixtures are standard in these rigs
(Chainsmokers WWJ: 60 Elation CuePix WW2 blinders + 41 GLP JDC1; Zedd: 64
GLP X4 Bar 20 doubling as the strobe element). Energy state: documented as
the drop/peak "release." Big-room carries markedly higher blinder counts
than any documented tech-house rig.

**8. Movement.** *Period in bars: not countable without footage.*
[INFERRED] Movers are cued to phrase boundaries via console effect engines
(see item 10). Parked-vs-moving fraction during a held drop: not measured.

**9. Negative space.** *Not judgeable without footage; rough-thirds method
would be used.* [INFERRED] Tech-house school documented as higher negative
space (restraint); big-room as lower (fuller looks). No percentage or
thirds value can be assigned from the written record.

**10. Position change vs motion effect.** *Not countable without footage.*
[DOCUMENTED, general] grandMA3 "Phasers"/effects engines are used for
continuous waveform motion; position palettes/presets for snap moves; both
are triggered at phrase events (intro/verse/pre-chorus/bridge/outro
structure). Which phrase event triggers which: not documented per-show.

## PART 4 — DOCUMENTED-SOURCE FINDINGS

### Fixture counts / rig roles (arena-to-festival scale; club-scale numbers are NOT published for these artists)

**John Summit — MSG "Comfort in Chaos" (2024)** [DOCUMENTED, PLSN Sept
2024]: per LD/co-PD Jason Baeri, Ayrton Rivales "were the workhorse of the
rig with 50 on the floor, 100 in the air and a few more scattered as key
lights" (≈150 total); plus 20 Ayrton Dominos designed "as an omnipresent
guide light" (under-riser underglow + upstage silhouette/aerial); "a couple
of hundred pixel lines with hundreds of thousands of parameters." Control:
three active + one spare grandMA3 full-size (MA3 mode). Vendor Chicago-based
LEC; Ayrton/MA distributed by ACT Entertainment. Show ran 100+ songs across
5.5 hours in three acts; the rig was designed to "expand and contract over
time."

**Zedd "Orbit" (2019)** [DOCUMENTED, PLSN]: 88 Robe Pointe (beam), 64 GLP
X4 Bar 20 (strobe + pixel), 12 Mythos2, 42 MAC Aura XB (wash), 23 ETC
Source Four Par (crowd), 2 grandMA2 full, 3 MDG The One + 2 base hazers.
Video: 205 ROE CB8 panels.

**Chainsmokers "Memories Do Not Open" (2017)** [DOCUMENTED, Production Club
case study; corroborated by Ayrton/SoundLightUp]: "The nearly 100,000
pound lighting design … contained: 54 VL4000 4k BeamWashes, 84 CP Sharpys,
18 Ayrton MagicDot R, 84 Ayrton MagicPanel 602s, 42 Ayrton MagicPanel FXs,
126 Solaris Flares, and 32 GLP Impression X4 Bars." Per-pod: 14 Solaris
Flares, 21 MagicPanels, 2 audience-scanning lasers. 36-city arena tour. (A
separate one-off festival build with the Backstreet Boys used different
counts — do not conflate.)

**Chainsmokers "World War Joy" (2019)** [DOCUMENTED, PLSN]: 2 grandMA2
full; 32 VL4000 BeamWash; 30 PRG Icon Edge; 45 Claypaky B-Eye K20; 16
Sharpy; 40 Robe Spiky; 41 GLP JDC1; 10 GLP X4; 10 GLP X4 Bar 20; 60 Elation
CuePix WW2 blinders; 16 Chroma-Q Color Force 12; 55 Solaris Flare; 2 base +
8 Radiance hazers. Note: WWJ was transitioning to a live-band show, not a
pure DJ set.

### LD statements on operating philosophy

**Zedd LD Stevie Hernandez** [DOCUMENTED, PLSN]: "It's a very cue-heavy
show. This is also the first tour we used timecode. The past tours and all
other shows and festivals were all manually operated in all departments."
Timecode was layered on only for elements too fast to hit manually.

**Robe case study, Zedd "True Colors"** [DOCUMENTED]: reviewers assumed the
show was timecoded "but it's run completely live!" (True Colors rig used
64 Robe Pointes vs Orbit's 88.)

**Chainsmokers WWJ LD Josh Beard** [DOCUMENTED, PLSN]: the show was "pretty
heavy on timecode," which "let me focus on calling spots."

**Brad Schiller, Lighting&Sound America, "The Basics of Busking" (Dec
2024)** [DOCUMENTED] — the clearest written statement of the operating
discipline this engine should emulate:

- **Restraint:** "Utilize restraint by not using everything for every song
  while also creating a dynamic and changing set of looks."
- **Repetition:** repeat a specific look when its musical section repeats
  (e.g., recreate the same chorus look each chorus).
- **Toolkit:** three combinable stacks — a **position** stack, a
  **color/color-combination** stack (organized by fixture type or physical
  location), and a **gobo/effect** stack — plus premade speed-adjustable
  chases (dimmer chase, ballyhoo, color chase) with tap-to-tempo, and a
  "get-out-of-jail-free" transitional cue that kills chases/effects and
  leaves stage light for a clean reset.
- **Musical structure:** anticipate changes via intro/verse/chorus/ending
  structure and time signature by counting beats.

**Rock-tour cue-count benchmarks (CONTEXT ONLY — NOT house, and NOT
independently verified):** a PLSN roundtable cited an LD manually firing
~600 cues/night (Fleetwood Mac) and ~97% timecode-fired cues (Mötley Crüe).
*These figures could not be re-verified against a named primary source and
are rock tours; do not hardcode.*

**DJ-specific reality** [DOCUMENTED]: **No published integer cue/look/song
count exists for John Summit, Dom Dolla, or Chris Lake shows.**
Improvisational DJ sets favor rule-based/busked lighting because timecoding
every track requires locking the setlist — the opposite of an agile DJ set
(multiple LD/operator sources).

**Haze is the enabling precondition:** beams and lasers are "almost
completely invisible without an atmospheric hazer" [DOCUMENTED, multiple].
Rigs carry multiple dedicated hazers (Zedd: 5; Chainsmokers WWJ: 10). At
EDC Las Vegas's kineticFIELD main stage "there were more than 60 fog/haze
machines present at main stage to ensure sufficient effects support for the
lasers" (Laserworld USA via PR Newswire). Haze is run before doors, not
fired during peaks.

### Design philosophy (restraint / contrast / color)

**Anyma (melodic techno, adjacent to the tech-house school)**
[DOCUMENTED, Designoor]: "a study in restraint … every four or eight bars
something shifted, never randomly." Control model: "Timecode provides the
spine while live faders keep the skin warm." Haze "managed like a paint
medium."

**Club-technique consensus (generic trade writeups)** [DOCUMENTED]: through
a build, "tighten everything — narrow the beams, raise intensity, speed the
movement, drop the colour toward white. On the drop, release: full strobe
or blinder, wide beams, hard colour. The contrast is what the crowd feels."
Warning that running everything at maximum "all night" numbs the room
within ~20 minutes.

**Chainsmokers big-room** [DOCUMENTED, Production Club]: "The creative
drive behind the design of World War Joy was the juxtaposition of beauty
and darkness. That is also reflected in the use of color throughout the
show." — i.e., color deployed as narrative contrast rather than restraint.

**Finding:** In the available written record the two schools are
**indistinguishable on every footage-dependent statistic (1, 4, 5, 8, 9,
10).** The only robustly documented differences: (a) big-room carries
larger blinder/strobe/video fixture counts and treats color as narrative
contrast, and (b) tech-house/techno design language explicitly emphasizes
restraint and monochrome. Any per-bar behavioral difference between the
schools is currently **unproven** and must not be assumed by the engine.

## PART 7 — OPEN QUESTIONS (require rig access, a programmer, or footage + stopwatch)

- **Look-change cadence in bars**, per energy state (BREAK/BUILD/DROP/
  PEAK/OUTRO) and per school — requires frame-counting pro-shot sets
  (Cercle, Boiler Room, Tomorrowland/EDC/Ultra official). This is the top
  gap.
- **Simultaneous "hues live" counts**, including the maximum sustained
  >4-bar count — the single most valuable calibration column, entirely
  unsourced.
- **Whether intensity pulses every quarter note during drops**, or only on
  phrase downbeats, or not at all. Documented evidence leans toward
  phrase/downbeat, but a counted fraction of DROP/PEAK time is needed.
- **Pre-drop dark duration in beats** and how often builds actually end in a
  hard cut vs a roll-through.
- **Laser moments per hour and minimum spacing** — no published figure
  exists for any in-scope artist; needs a laser programmer interview or
  counted footage. Also a hard club-vs-festival contrast.
- **Strobe burst durations and gaps** in seconds, and countable rate.
- **Mover period in bars**, and the parked-vs-moving fraction during a held
  drop.
- **Negative-space fraction** (rig dark at any moment) by energy state,
  even to rough thirds.
- **Exact cue counts and timecode-% for in-scope DJ shows** (John Summit,
  Dom Dolla, Chris Lake) — not published anywhere located; would require
  direct programmer access. The nearest documented anchor is the Zedd
  model: fully manual/busked base with timecode added only for sub-frame
  accents.

*Note on footage limits that will affect any future logging effort:*
pro-shot EDM uploads are heavily color-graded and rapidly cut, LED-wall
content frequently dominates the frame, and the rig is often off-camera
during the exact drop moments that matter — so even a human logger should
expect many sets to be classed "unusable" rather than forced into a table.
